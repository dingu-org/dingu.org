# Voice

How dingu.org writes. Four concepts. Settle an argument by pointing at a pair, not at an adjective.

The site is a tree with branches on it. That is the whole metaphor, and it is already spent. Everything below exists to keep it working instead of taking over.

---

## Concept: Nature is vocabulary, not atmosphere

**Characteristics:** functional, unsentimental, literal

**What this means:** nature words earn their place by doing a job. `pruned` is a status meaning someone killed the project on purpose. `dormant` means nothing has happened in a while. They are accurate before they are pretty. A nature word that only sets a mood gets cut.

The rule that keeps this honest: **one nature image per sentence.** A tree with branches on it is one image, not two. Stack a second into the same sentence and the metaphor stops describing anything and starts performing.

**Where the metaphor holds.** A tree grows its branches, and that is the claim the site now makes: these projects come from dingu.org, alone or with the people it works with. Keep `branch` for what a project is, and keep the verb about origin — cultivated, planted — not about hosting or owning.

**Do**
- "pruned"
- "dormant"
- "Every project here is cultivated by or with dingu.org. Each planted small."
- "projects planted small, each moving at its own pace"

**Don't**
- "Tend your digital garden."
- "Every project begins as a seed."
- "Rooted in intention, grown with care."
- "We believe in slow, deliberate growth."
- "One tree, many branches." (retired: the image alone, saying nothing about what the page lists)

---

## Concept: Plain

**Characteristics:** short, lowercase, unhedged

**What this means:** the shortest true version wins. Labels are lowercase with no closing period. Prose is sentence case. Nothing is softened into a suggestion, and nothing is announced before it is said.

**Do**
- "growing"
- "what grows under dingu.org"
- "learning by making"

**Don't**
- "Currently Growing"
- "Welcome to dingu.org, home of my side projects."
- "A curated collection of things I've been working on."

---

## Concept: Concrete

**Characteristics:** verb-led, specific, adjective-free

**What this means:** name what the thing does to the world. The four project descriptions carry zero adjectives between them, and that is the target. If a line would survive on someone else's site, it has not said anything.

**Do**
- "quieting the inbox"
- "feeding a calm mind"
- "Some grow into tools you can use today."

**Don't**
- "a more mindful approach to email"
- "thoughtfully designed reading experiences"
- "a growing suite of useful tools"

---

## Concept: Honest about state

**Characteristics:** measured, plain about gaps, never breathless

**What this means:** the status vocabulary already admits that things stall and get killed. The writing does the same. Nothing is promised before it works, and a dead thing is named as dead rather than parked in a polite holding pattern.

**Do**
- "dormant"
- "pruned"
- "growing"

**Don't**
- "coming soon"
- "on hiatus"
- "sunset" / "deprecated"
- "Exciting things ahead."

---

## What this voice is not

- Nature-mystical, not just nature-referencing. No wisdom, no seasons as life lessons, no invitations to slow down.
- Restrained, not cold. Short is the point; withholding is not.
- Personal site, not a person talking. Rarely first person, never chatty, no exclamation marks.
- Understated, not falsely modest. It does not apologise for itself either.

---

## Conventions

**Casing.** Statuses, project descriptions, and the meta description are lowercase. Prose is sentence case. Section titles are sentence case and render exactly as written, with no CSS transform.

**Punctuation.** No terminal period on labels or descriptors. No exclamation marks. No em dashes or their stand-ins anywhere, in copy or in docs.

**Length.** Project descriptions: three to five words. Prose blocks: three sentences at most.

**Project descriptions name the thing.** A reader arriving at the index knows none of these projects, so the description says what it is, not what it feels like. `appointment assistant for WhatsApp`, not `booking appointments over WhatsApp`. Gerund-led lines read as mood next to a name they cannot place. One naming adjective is allowed when it does the work, as in `personal site and portfolio`.

**Signature words.** `grow`, `plant`, `prune`, `branch`, `alive`, `dormant`. A generic editing pass will read these as decorative metaphor and try to strip them. They stay. That is the whole voice.

**Never write "simple."** The site is simple, which is exactly why it must never say so. The same goes for `minimal`, `clean`, `thoughtful`, and `carefully crafted`. The design is the proof; the adjective only weakens it.

**Facts the copy must not get wrong.** dingu.org is an index. Any line implying the projects live under, run on, or originate from it is wrong, however well it scans.

**Where a project is hosted is not copy.** Some sit on `*.dingu.org` subdomains and some on unrelated domains. The list does not show addresses at all now; the visit link carries each one, so prose that explains the arrangement is spending a sentence on plumbing. Say what a branch is, not where it lives.

**Locale.** No clear signal in the shipped copy yet. Assume en-US until a spelling forces the question, then record the answer here.

## Testing a line

Read it against these in order. A no anywhere means rewrite, not soften.

1. Could this line sit on any other personal site? (If yes, it is not specific enough.)
2. Does every nature word do a job a plain word could not?
3. More than one nature image in any single sentence?
4. Any adjective making a claim the page does not already prove?
5. Does it imply dingu.org hosts or grew the projects rather than indexing them?
6. Shortest true version?
